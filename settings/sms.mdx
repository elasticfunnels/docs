Every text your brand sends takes the same path: the **Send SMS** step in an automation, the automatic
replies to STOP, HELP and START, and test sends from settings. First the compliance checks run, then the
text goes out from your own Twilio, Plivo or Telnyx account. Every attempt is logged, sent or not, with the reason.

You set the checks up in **Settings** -> **SMS**. The page has five tabs: **Compliance**, **Senders**,
**Suppression list**, **Consent lookup** and **Test send**.

<Warning>
ElasticFunnels does not give legal advice. Complying with the TCPA and state texting laws is your
brand's responsibility. The checks below help you catch common problems; they cannot tell whether a
person really agreed to your texts.
</Warning>

## Before your first text

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect your SMS provider">
    Connect your Twilio, Plivo or Telnyx account in **Integrations**. See [Twilio](/integrations/twilio),
    [Plivo](/integrations/plivo) or [Telnyx](/integrations/telnyx).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a sender">
    In **Settings** -> **SMS** -> **Senders**, click **Add sender** and pick a number or messaging
    service from your provider account. Then click **Configure webhooks**. See [Senders](#senders).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Accept the marketing consent statement">
    On the **Compliance** tab, read the statement and accept it. Until someone on the brand does,
    marketing texts are skipped. Transactional texts are not affected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send yourself a test">
    On the **Test send** tab, text your own number and check the result. See [Test send](#test-send).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What happens to each text

A text ends in one of three ways, and the log shows which:

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| Sent | It passed the checks and went to Twilio. The log then shows **Sent**, **Delivered**, **Undelivered** or **Failed** as Twilio reports back. |
| Waiting | Quiet hours hold it. It goes out at the next time the recipient's local hours allow. |
| Skipped | A check stopped it. The log shows why. |

A skipped text is a normal result. In an automation, the run goes on to the next step.

## Compliance tab

<img src="/images/settings/sms-compliance.png" alt="Settings, SMS, Compliance tab: the always-enforced checks, Warn or Strict, and the marketing consent statement" />

### Always enforced

These checks run on every text, in warn mode and in strict mode. You cannot turn them off.

- **STOP, HELP and START.** A reply of STOP suppresses the number, records the opt-out and sends one
  confirmation (only the first time). START lifts the suppression. HELP gets your help reply.
- **The suppression list.** A number on it gets no texts at all, transactional ones included.
- **Opt-outs reported by your provider.** When the provider refuses a text because the person opted out
  (Twilio error 21610, Plivo 200, Telnyx 40300), the number goes on the suppression list.
- **Invalid numbers** are skipped.
- **States you excluded** under **Where you send** get no marketing texts.
- **A sender must be set up.** Without one, nothing is sent.

The words that count as each keyword:

| Keyword | Also matches |
|---|---|
| STOP | STOPALL, UNSUBSCRIBE, UNSUB, CANCEL, END, QUIT, REVOKE, OPT OUT |
| HELP | INFO |
| START | UNSTOP, YES, Y |

### Warn or strict

Consent, state rules, frequency caps, message content and sender registration are checked on every
text too. **When a check finds a problem** decides what happens next:

| | Warn (default) | Strict |
|---|---|---|
| A problem is found | Logged as a warning, and the text still sends | The text is skipped, with the reason |
| The frequency cap is reached | Logged as a warning, and the text still sends | The text waits for the next allowed time |

What these checks look at:

- **Consent:** whether the number has an SMS consent record. A purchase records consent for
  transactional texts. A ticked SMS box on a checkout records consent for marketing texts (see
  [Consent from your checkout](#consent-from-your-checkout)).
- **State rules:** extra rules in the recipient's state, for example Florida needs written consent.
- **Frequency caps:** by default, at most 3 marketing texts per person in 24 hours. A state limit
  applies when it is lower.
- **Content:** restricted topics carriers filter (such as cannabis or CBD, gambling, alcohol), public link
  shorteners like bit.ly, too many capital letters, long messages, special characters that raise the
  cost, and promotional words in a transactional text.
- **Sender registration:** whether the sender has 10DLC or toll-free registration.
- **Country:** only US numbers can be texted for now.

### Marketing consent statement

In both modes, marketing texts are skipped until someone with access to the brand accepts the
statement. It says the brand only texts people who agreed to it, in writing where state law requires
it, and honors every opt-out. The tab shows who accepted it and when. **Withdraw** turns marketing
texts off again.

### Quiet hours

<img src="/images/settings/sms-quiet-hours.png" alt="Quiet hours on the Compliance tab, the optional send window and the frequency caps" />

**Hold texts during quiet hours** is on by default and applies to marketing texts.

- Texts go out between 8am and 8pm in the recipient's local time, read from the number's area code.
- Pennsylvania: 9am to 7pm, and no texts on Sundays or federal holidays. Connecticut: 9am to 8pm.
- When the state is unknown, the text waits until those hours are open in every mainland US time
  zone.
- A held text goes out at the next open time, in 15-minute steps. If it is still held when it expires
  (48 hours by default), it is skipped with **Quiet hours lasted past the send deadline**.

You can turn quiet hours off. Texts then go out at any hour with a warning in the log, and keeping to
state texting hours is up to you.

**Your own send window** narrows the hours further, for example 10am to 6pm on weekdays. Leave it
empty to use quiet hours alone. No days selected means every day.

**Frequency caps** set the most marketing texts one person gets: **Per day** (3 by default) and an
optional **Per week**.

### Automatic replies

The texts sent back for STOP, HELP and an opt-in. Leave a field empty to use the default:

| Field | Default |
|---|---|
| **Brand name in replies** | Your brand name. Carriers expect every text to say who it is from. |
| **STOP reply** | `{brand}: You are unsubscribed and will receive no more texts. Reply START to resubscribe.` |
| **HELP reply** | `{brand}: For help, contact us. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to cancel.` |
| **Opt-in confirmation** | Sent when someone replies START: `{brand}: You are subscribed to texts again. Msg frequency varies. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply HELP for help, STOP to cancel.` |

### Where you send and state texting rules

**Where you send** lets you exclude states you do not want to text at all. Excluded states get no
marketing texts, in both modes. The quick presets add the **Strictest**, **Strict** or **Moderate**
states to the list; save to apply. **Skip numbers when we cannot tell the state** is off by default.

**State texting rules** lists what is checked for each state: its strictness level, hours and daily
limits, and what it requires (written consent, seller registration, an existing relationship). Tick
**Registered** once you have registered where a state requires it, or **Exclude** to stop texting it.

### Webhook addresses

The **Status** and **Replies** addresses Twilio calls to report delivery and pass on replies. Each
brand has its own address with a secret token in it. **Configure webhooks** on the Senders tab sets
them on your Twilio number for you; they are shown here so you can check by hand.

Calls without the right token are refused. When the Twilio integration also holds your account's Auth
Token, each call is checked against Twilio's signature as well.

The addresses shown are the Twilio ones. For a Plivo or Telnyx sender, **Configure webhooks** sets that
provider's own address, and every call is checked against the provider's signature (the Plivo Auth Token
or the Telnyx public key). See [Plivo](/integrations/plivo#replies-and-delivery-reports) and
[Telnyx](/integrations/telnyx#replies-and-delivery-reports).

## Senders

<img src="/images/settings/sms-senders.png" alt="The Senders tab with a default local number and a toll-free number, their registration and who answers STOP and HELP" />

The numbers this brand texts from, on your own Twilio, Plivo or Telnyx account. Plivo Powerpacks and Telnyx
messaging profiles can be senders too, next to Twilio messaging services.

1. Click **Add sender**, choose the **Twilio account** (a Twilio integration connected to the brand)
   and a **Number**. The list shows the numbers and messaging services on that account.
2. Turn on **Default sender** for the one used when a step does not pick a sender.
3. Turn on **Twilio already answers STOP/HELP for this number** only if Twilio Advanced Opt-Out is set
   up for it. ElasticFunnels then records the reply and the opt-out without sending its own reply.
4. Save, then click **Configure webhooks** so replies and delivery reports reach ElasticFunnels.

**Registration** shows the 10DLC or toll-free status Twilio reports: **Registered**, **Pending**,
**Rejected**, **Not required** or **Unknown**. Click **Refresh** to ask Twilio again. Carriers block or
filter texts from unregistered numbers, so register in the Twilio Console before you send real
traffic.

A paused sender is not used for any text.

## Suppression list

<img src="/images/settings/sms-suppression-list.png" alt="The Suppression list with a number that replied STOP, a carrier opt-out and one added by hand" />

Numbers this brand never texts, transactional texts included. A number lands here when the person
replies STOP, when Twilio reports a carrier opt-out, or when you add it.

- **Add number** blocks a number by hand, with the reason **Added by hand** or **Complaint**. Include
  the country code, for example +1 for the US.
- **Lift** lets the number get texts again. A person who texts START lifts it too.
- Search by number, and switch to **All, with lifted** to see past entries.

## Consent lookup

<img src="/images/settings/sms-consent-lookup.png" alt="Consent lookup for one number: its state, suppression status, the consent record with the checkbox wording and page URL, and the texts it got" />

Type a phone number and click **Look up** to see everything the brand holds about it:

- the state it was placed in, and whether it is suppressed,
- each consent record: opted in or out, marketing or transactional, where it came from, the wording the
  person saw and the page URL,
- the texts sent to it and the replies from it.

**Export JSON** downloads all of it as one file, for a carrier or legal request.

## Test send

<img src="/images/settings/sms-test-send.png" alt="The Test send tab: your number, sender, type and message, with the segment count" />

Sends one text through the same checks as a real send and shows what they decided: sent, waiting or
skipped, with the reason and any warnings. The text is real and goes out from your Twilio account, so
only send to a number you own. Under the message you see the character count, the number of segments
and the encoding.

A marketing test is skipped while the consent statement is not accepted.

## The SMS log

<img src="/images/settings/sms-log-messages.png" alt="Settings, Logs, SMS: a text waiting for quiet hours, one skipped because the number is suppressed, and delivered texts with a warning" />

**Settings** -> **Logs** -> **SMS** lists every text the brand sent or tried to send: its status, type,
source, what the checks decided, and when. Click a row to see the full text, the segments, the reason
it was skipped, any warnings and Twilio's error.

<img src="/images/settings/sms-log-replies.png" alt="The Replies view of the SMS log: a STOP reply and a question from a customer" />

Switch to **Replies** to see the texts people sent back to your numbers, with the keyword when it was
STOP, HELP or START.

### Why a text was skipped

| Reason | What to do |
|---|---|
| Number is on the suppression list | The person replied STOP, Twilio reported an opt-out, or someone added the number. Only the person texting START, or a lift, changes it. |
| Marketing statement not accepted | Accept the statement on the Compliance tab. |
| No active sender | Add a sender on the Senders tab. |
| Invalid phone number | The number is not a valid mobile number. Check the variable the step uses. |
| State excluded in your settings | You chose not to text that state under **Where you send**. |
| Quiet hours lasted past the send deadline | The text waited too long for open hours. |
| Twilio reports the number opted out | The person opted out with their carrier. The number is now suppressed. |
| Twilio refused the message | The error code in the log shows why. |

In strict mode, the warning reasons (no consent, state rule, content, sender registration, country) also
skip the text.

## Consent from your checkout

When a shopper ticks the SMS opt-in box (`allow_sms`) on a checkout or upsell page, the order stores
marketing consent together with the wording next to the box and the page URL. Both show in
**Consent lookup**. A purchase records consent for transactional texts. See
[SMS and email opt-in boxes](/pages/privacy-consent#sms-and-email-opt-in-boxes-on-checkout-and-upsell-pages).
