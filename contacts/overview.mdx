<Note>
Contacts is switched on for your brand. The sidebar entry is still called **Customers**; the screen it opens is titled **Contacts**. If your Customers screen still shows one row per email address, your brand has not been moved yet and the old screen works as before.
</Note>

A contact is one person in your brand. Every order, abandoned checkout and form fill is matched to a contact, so a buyer who used two email addresses or later added a phone number stays one record instead of two.

## The Contacts list
{/* SCREENSHOT: Contacts list with the All, Customers and Leads tabs · desktop */}

Open **Customers** in the sidebar. The list shows one row per contact with their email, name, phone, country, orders, revenue and last order date.

- **Tabs.** **All contacts**, **Customers** (anyone who has paid) and **Leads** (people who gave you their details but have not bought yet).
- **Search.** Type a name or an email address and press Enter.
- **Sort.** Newest or oldest order, highest or lowest revenue, most or fewest orders, newest or oldest contact.
- **Reload.** The reload button at the right of the search box fetches the list again, so a contact created a moment ago shows up without refreshing the page.

Click a contact's email to open their record. To bring in people from a spreadsheet, click **Import contacts**: see [Importing contacts](/contacts/import).

## A contact's record
{/* SCREENSHOT: Contact record with the Contact tab open · desktop */}

Each contact has its own address in the app (`/customers/<contact id>`), which keeps working if you change the contact's email. Older links that open a customer by email still work and move to the contact's address.

The record has these tabs:

| Tab | What it shows |
|-----|---------------|
| **Overview** | Lifetime stats, orders and transactions, as before. |
| **Contact** | Name, location, timezone, every email and phone the contact has used (the primary one is marked), tags, and where the contact first came from. |
| **Consent** | The email and SMS marketing status, and the history of opt-ins and opt-outs with their source. |
| **Merges** | Every contact merged into this one, with an **Undo** button while undo is still possible. |
| **Sales & Refunds**, **Products** | The contact's orders, refunds and the products they bought. |
| **Sessions** | The visits that led to an order. See [Sessions](#sessions) below. |
| **Subscriptions** | The contact's subscriptions, for users who may view orders. |
| **Transactions** | Gateway attempts, statuses and amounts. |
| **CRM** | CRM entries linked to the contact. |

The order tabs are looked up by the contact's email, so they show only to users who can see emails in full (see [Who sees emails and phone numbers](#who-sees-emails-and-phone-numbers)).

## Sessions
{/* SCREENSHOT: Contact record with the Sessions tab open · desktop */}

The **Sessions** tab lists the visits that led to an order, newest first. Each row shows:

- **Started**: the date and time the visit began, in your own timezone.
- **Session**: the funnel name, or the landing page name when the visit was not in a funnel, with the session code underneath. Click it to open the session with every page and event in a new tab.
- **Traffic source**: the ad platform, UTM source or referring site, with the campaign underneath. A tracked visit with no source shows **Direct**.
- **Device**: mobile, desktop or tablet, and the visitor's country.
- **Activity**: how many pages the visit viewed and how long it lasted. **Not tracked** means no page views were recorded for that visit.
- **Session value**, **Products** and **Orders** for the visit.

Click the **Started**, **Activity**, **Session value**, **Products** or **Orders** heading to sort by it.

## Activity and lifetime value
{/* SCREENSHOT: Contact record side panel with the lifetime value and Timeline · desktop */}

The side panel of the record shows the contact's **LTV** (lifetime value) and their activity.

- **LTV** is the total of the contact's real orders across every email and visitor profile linked to them: purchases minus refunds and chargebacks. Test orders are not counted.
- **Timeline** lists what the contact did, newest first, in plain words such as **Became a known contact**, **Viewed a page**, **Opened the checkout**, **Purchased** or **Accepted an upsell**, each with one short detail line. Switch between **Brand TZ** and **My TZ** to read the times in the brand's timezone or yours.
- Your team opening a contact's record is not recorded as activity, so the timeline and the first and last seen dates show only what the contact did.
- **Touchpoints** shows the first and last ad platform, campaign and UTM source that brought the contact in.

## Editing a contact
{/* SCREENSHOT: Edit contact drawer · desktop */}

On the **Contact** tab, click **Edit** to change the first name, last name, country, region, postal code, timezone and tags.

A field you edit by hand is **locked**: new orders and imports no longer overwrite it, and it shows a **Locked** badge. To let incoming data update it again, hover the **Locked** badge and click **Unlock**.

If someone else changed the contact while you were editing, saving tells you so; reload the record and edit again.

Email addresses and phone numbers are not edited here. They come from the contact's orders and forms.

## Who sees emails and phone numbers
{/* SCREENSHOT: Contacts list with masked emails · desktop */}

Seeing a contact's email, phone, name and postal code in full needs the **See unmasked** permission on the **Contact details** row of a role. Without it the Contacts screens mask them (`j***@g***.com`, `***4567`, `J***`), and the order tabs that are looked up by email stay hidden. The same masking applies to audience member lists and exports, and to the audience sample and recipients list of an email campaign.

- Brand owners always have it.
- Organization admins who could already see customers get it automatically.
- For any other role, tick **See unmasked** on the **Contact details** row in **Organization → Roles**.

Editing names or postal codes also needs this permission, so nobody overwrites a value they cannot see.
